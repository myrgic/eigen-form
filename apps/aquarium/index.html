Aquarium
the agent-substrate engine's first configuration — momentum, heat, chemistry, and three populations sharing one tank

The equations, in words. Water is an incompressible velocity field (projected divergence-free every step) carrying heat by advection; a heater sources temperature, a Boussinesq term (beta·ΔT) turns warmer water buoyant, and the surface loses heat back toward room temperature. Ammonia (from fish) becomes nitrite then nitrate through two Monod-limited, logistically-growing bacteria populations confined to the filter media — the nitrogen cycle a real fresh tank runs, sped up by a declared time-scale multiplier so it plays out in minutes rather than weeks. Fish are boids on their own density/velocity field, reading the current for rheotaxis and a nutrient gradient for food; plankton are a physarum weld on that same nutrient field — they grow it, fish graze it down. Bubbles rise at a radius-dependent terminal speed with a random walk and pop at the surface. Plants are the one hand-stepped exception: small Verlet chains reading the current and the light field, not a declared population. The CPU reference implementation (engine/cpu.js) is the instrument of record — the WebGL2 backend is a checked projection of it, verified within a declared tolerance (twin.html), never trusted past that tolerance (docs/aquarium-design.md).
